PROJECT 04 • AUDIT ANALYTICS • EXCEL

Finding the story hidden inside thousands of expense transactions.

Travel and expense data can look like rows, dates, merchants, and dollar amounts. My job was to figure out what those transactions were actually telling us about risk, policy compliance, and data quality.

The Challenge

Large datasets do not come with the interesting transactions highlighted for you.

I analyzed company-wide travel and expense data as part of internal audit work designed to evaluate policy compliance, financial controls, and unusual activity.

The work required more than simply filtering for large dollar amounts. Different transaction patterns could point to very different questions.

The analysis had to separate ordinary business activity from transactions that deserved a closer look.

What I Tested

Build the right questions into the data.

01

Travel Timing

Reviewed hotel check-in and check-out timing, flight dates, one-way travel patterns, and other date-related exceptions.

02

Policy Exceptions

Evaluated transactions for activity such as unauthorized international travel and other potential policy violations.

03

Duplicate Activity

Looked for duplicate dates, overlapping activity, and transactions that could indicate duplicate or unusual expense behavior.

04

Employee Status

Reviewed expense activity associated with inactive employees or situations where card ownership and employee status raised additional questions.

05

Data Quality

Identified situations where merchant information, expense classifications, or supporting fields did not clearly reflect the underlying transaction.

06

Payment Patterns

Reviewed issues involving multiple cards, mileage activity, clearing processes, and other patterns requiring additional investigation.

What the Analysis Revealed

Sometimes the finding is the transaction. Sometimes it is the data itself.

Policy & Process Findings

Analysis identified transactions and booking activity that required additional review, including travel patterns, unauthorized bookings, and situations that did not align cleanly with established policy expectations.

Data Quality Findings

The audit also surfaced issues with fields such as merchant information and expense classifications, highlighting how weak source data can make financial oversight and policy monitoring harder.

That distinction mattered because not every exception meant an employee had done something wrong.

Sometimes the real issue was a process, system, classification, or control that made the transaction difficult to interpret correctly.

Good audit analytics means asking what the data shows before deciding what the data means.

Business Impact

From transactions to conversations and action plans.

Risk Visibility

Helped surface policy exceptions, unusual activity, and data-quality concerns that required additional business review.

Stakeholder Discussion

Findings supported conversations with managers and business stakeholders to understand root causes, clarify processes, and resolve open questions.

Process Improvement

Audit results contributed to follow-up actions, knowledge-transfer discussions, and improvements designed to strengthen oversight.
